Interconnect topology reference

What "good" scale-out fabric looks like — the five knobs that determine whether the cluster performs at its silicon

Fat tree — non-blocking leaf-spine

Two-tier Clos: every leaf switch connects to every spine (full mesh), and every host connects to a leaf. Multiple equal-cost paths between any two hosts, so no single link failure partitions the fabric. "Non-blocking" means the total leaf uplink bandwidth to spines equals its total downlink bandwidth to hosts — nothing bottlenecks.

Rail alignment — each GPU's NIC on its own switch plane

GPU N in every node connects to Rail-N switch and only Rail-N switch. All-to-all traffic on rail K stays on rail K — no cross-rail congestion. Non-rail-aligned fabric funnels all 8 GPUs' traffic through one shared switch, where MoE all-to-all bursts contend at the leaf uplinks. Rail alignment is the single most under-appreciated determinant of AI cluster goodput.

Switch radix — port count sets the max cluster size per tier

Radix = number of ports on a single switch. In a non-blocking 2-tier fat tree, half the radix goes down to hosts and half goes up to spines, so max non-blocking hosts ≈ (radix / 2)² × spines. Higher radix means fewer switch tiers, fewer hops, lower latency, and less cabling. Modern IB / Ethernet switches ship 64–128 ports at 400G+ specifically to enable single-tier or two-tier fabrics at AI scale.

GPU-to-NIC ratio — dedicated NIC per GPU is the AI norm

The scale-out NIC connects a GPU to the fabric. AI training and MoE inference need each GPU to move data at line rate independently of its neighbors, so the norm is 1 NIC per GPU (typically 400G NDR or 800G XDR). Shared NICs halve or quarter the effective per-GPU cross-node bandwidth — fine for enterprise VM clusters where GPUs run isolated jobs, ruinous for tensor-parallel or MoE workloads.

Oversubscription ratio — leaf uplink vs downlink bandwidth

At each switch tier, the ratio of downlink bandwidth (to hosts) to uplink bandwidth (to the next tier) determines how much cross-tier traffic the fabric can carry. 1:1 = non-blocking (every host can talk to every other host at full line rate simultaneously). 2:1 or worse = the uplinks bottleneck when many flows go cross-tier — fine for enterprise workloads with locality, ruinous for training where every step has all-reduce that must traverse the full bisection.
